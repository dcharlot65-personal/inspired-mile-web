# Play content: where every title, name and quotation comes from

_Generated by `scripts/lang-content/provenance.mjs`. Do not edit: change the data and run it again._

This covers the playground and story pages, the trivia bank (200 questions), the quote challenges and story mode, in the fifteen languages other than English.

## What was written by whom

| Content | Who wrote it | What checked it |
|---|---|---|
| Titles of works, names of characters, people and places, as trivia options (458 of the 800 options) | Nobody: filled from the names table by `build.mjs` (Wikidata labels; names read in a Wikipedia article; otherwise the English name) | `play-areas.test.ts`: each is its source's name, or a form listed below with its reason |
| Quotations in the quote challenges | Nobody: copied from Wikisource pages by `wikisource.py take` | `verify-challenges.mjs` (each line must be in the evidence file of its source) |
| Shakespeare's lines inside questions, options and story scenes | Left in English on purpose, as on the cards | `play-areas.test.ts` (quotation unchanged) |
| Names inside 50 other options (Montague, Escalus, Cesario, "Brutus and Cassius", "Goneril, Regan, and Cordelia", …), not filled from the table | Typed in each language's file; every name in them checked against a source | `play-areas.test.ts` (handNameProblems): each is a form a Wikidata label or alias, a names-evidence line or a place's label gives, or the English name; see **Name options written in each language** |
| Trivia questions and the 292 options that are sentences or common nouns; the playground and story text | A language model (Claude), in one pass per language | Mechanical tests; `check.mjs` (names in questions against the table); Hunspell; back-translation by two translation models and an alignment check of the answers: see **Checks** below |

**Not verified by a native speaker.** Nothing in the last row has been read by one. The checks below find what a tool can find; a back-translation that reads well is not proof that a sentence is idiomatic.

## Sources

- **Wikidata** (labels, aliases and sitelinks; CC0). Fetched 2026-11-16T17:18Z by `fetch-wikidata.py` through the public API (`wbgetentities`); stored whole in `scripts/lang-content/wikidata-labels.json`. 238 items.
- **Wikipedia article titles**, as Wikidata's sitelinks give them, where an item has no label in a language. A title is a name, not text.
- **Wikipedia articles** (text CC BY-SA 4.0; only names are taken from them, never text), fetched 2026-11-16 by `wikipedia.py`: the articles on the plays, on Shakespeare and on the Globe, one revision each, read through `index.php` (the API was refusing this address with HTTP 429). A name Wikidata has no label for is taken from an article only where the article is the play's own (its Wikidata sitelink; for a person, place or work, the articles `evidence.mjs` lists), and the name stands there as the character: in the cast list, the plot, or with the English beside it ("愛麗兒（Ariel）", "Пигва (Peter Quince)"), not as part of somebody else's name (Барри Эдгар, Laurence Olivier, アーロン・ヒル) and not among film credits. Each such name is a line of `scripts/lang-content/names-evidence.tsv`: the item, the language, the name, the URL of the article revision, the date, and the words around it, which must be one of those sound occurrences. 361 lines. `node scripts/lang-content/evidence.mjs audit` checks every line against the cached revision; `play-areas.test.ts` checks the article and the recorded words of every line. Before 2026-11-16 these rules did not exist, and the lines were only required to hold the word: an independent checker found six that named somebody else, and the audit 48 that broke a rule (see **Checks**). A line accepted by hand despite a rule is listed in `evidence.mjs` (ACCEPTED) with the reason.
- **Wikidata labels of three places** (CC0), fetched 2026-11-17T00:27Z by `fetch-places.py` (`wikidata-places.json`): Kent (Q23298), Cornwall (Q23148), Gloucester (Q170497). The Earl of Kent, the Duke of Cornwall and the Earl of Gloucester are named after them; in a language with no article on *King Lear* the place's label is the source for the name in their title.
- **Chinese**: labels and titles in Traditional characters were converted to Simplified by MediaWiki's own converter on zh.wikipedia.org (`zh-simplify.py`; marked `zh-hans:from` in the JSON); Chinese articles and Zhu Shenghao's translation were read as zh.wikipedia and zh.wikisource serve them in Simplified (`variant=zh-cn` / `zh-hans`).

## Names

Every name the game's content uses, with its Wikidata item. The name in each language, and how it was arrived at, is in `docs/language/data/content-names.tsv` (one row per name, two columns per language).

How a name is arrived at, in this order: a pick (`picks.tsv`), which must be found in a source; the Wikidata label; where there is none (or, for a work, only a copy of the English), the title of that language's Wikipedia article; where the English label says more than the game does ("Prince Hamlet" for "Hamlet"), the matching part of the label, in languages that put a title before a name; otherwise the English name is left as it is. **A name is never transliterated by guesswork**: in the languages not written in Latin letters, a name no source has stays in Latin letters.

### Name options written otherwise than the table

An option that is a name, written by hand. `play-areas.test.ts` accepts one only if it is the table's name shortened (a label's disambiguator dropped), the table's name with a title or a common noun translated around it ("El rey Lear", "Soneto 116"), or listed in `trivia-option-forms.tsv` with its reason. Reasons: **title** (a title or a common noun translated, the name as the table has it); **inflected** (the case the question needs); **number** (an ordinal or numeral the language writes); **script** (every option of that question in Latin letters, because a lone label in the language's script would have shown which option is right). A name typed by the translator in a script no source has is not among them: those were replaced by the table's name.

Why so many are dropped. What was searched, on Wikisource unless said otherwise: *Hamlet* in one translation per language (es, fr, de, it, pl, ru); *Romeo and Juliet* in French (Hugo), German (Schlegel), Italian (Rusconi), Spanish (Menéndez y Pelayo), Russian (Grigoryev) and Polish (Ulrich); *Julius Caesar* in French (Hugo), Italian (Rusconi) and Polish (Ulrich); *Macbeth* in Spanish (Menéndez y Pelayo), Italian (Rusconi) and Polish (Ulrich; Russian has Kroneberg's only in the pre-reform spelling, with letters a player cannot type, so it was not used); *The Merchant of Venice* in Spanish (Menéndez y Pelayo); *Othello* in Spanish (Menéndez y Pelayo), Italian (Rusconi), Polish (Ulrich), Russian (Weinberg) and German (Wieland's prose of 1766; German Wikisource has no Schlegel-Tieck text of the other plays); for Chinese, every one of Zhu Shenghao's translations zh.wikisource has (*Hamlet*, *The Tempest*, *A Midsummer Night's Dream*; the other links of its Shakespeare portal are pages not yet made); for Japanese, Tsubouchi Shōyō's *Romeo and Juliet* on Aozora Bunko (his *Hamlet* is still in preparation there and is not on ja.wikisource). A line not found whole in one line or verse of the source was left out, not stitched together (many prose translations of the 1880s abridge or paraphrase); one found was left out for Japanese because its words are archaic forms a player could not type (79, 'they stumble that run fast': 賢う徐うぢゃ。馳出す者は蹉躓くわい). Not searched, so no finding either way: the other works in these languages; Portuguese, whose Wikisource has the *Hamlet* of King Luís I (1877) and of Bulhão Pato (1879) only as page scans. Searched on 2026-11-16 for the languages with none (Wikisource search, and the pages read through wikisource.py): **Arabic**: ar.wikisource has one page of Shakespeare's maxims in Arabic, *حكم منتخبة من روايات شكسبير* in al-Muqtabas no. 6 (22 July 1906, unsigned, sent from Beirut; anonymous and published in 1906, so public domain at home and in the US), and two of its lines are lines of the bank (20, 'nothing either good or bad, but thinking makes it so'; 31, 'cowards die many times before their deaths'): fewer than the eight a language needs, so Arabic plays the English originals; its other Shakespeare pages are essays in al-Risala (1930s to 1950s), not translations of the plays. **Korean**: ko.wikisource has *하믈레트* (1920) with its transcription only begun, and a *맥베스* with no translator or edition named whose Korean reads as a machine translation (“이 끔찍한 위업에 대한 각 상병”): neither is a published translation that can be quoted. **Hindi, Vietnamese, Indonesian**: their Wikisources return no translation of a play by Shakespeare. **Portuguese**: pt.wikisource's *Otelo* index lists Rebelo da Silva (1856) and King Luís I (1885) with no transcribed text; Olavo Bilac's verse version of *Romeo and Juliet* III.5 (Bilac died 1918; public domain) is there but holds no line of the bank. **Thai**: th.wikisource has none of King Vajiravudh's translations (Rama VI, died 1925; *เวนิสวาณิช*, The Merchant of Venice, among them). The Vajirayana Digital Library (vajirayana.org) is the other public-domain library for Thai texts of that period, but its robots.txt disallows automated AI agents, so nothing was fetched from it: a person with that library or a printed edition can supply the lines, with their source.

The Japanese lines are in the old orthography of the 1933 edition (旧字旧仮名), as Aozora gives them; the missing words were chosen among those written the same today, so that a player can type them.
