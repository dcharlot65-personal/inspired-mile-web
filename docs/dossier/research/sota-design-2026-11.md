## Bottom line

The research found no shipped 2024-2026 game in which players write verse that code and a model score at scale, so Sonnet Man is aimed at an open space with no proven demand; the closest analogue, a party pack with a local AI judge, drew one Steam review and was delisted. The state of the art around it is clear. The card and word games that won in 2025-2026 were short (about three minutes a match), started in seconds, explained their scores, added content on a rhythm and kept monetisation visibly fair, and the habit leaders run one shared daily puzzle. Player hostility to AI that replaces human craft grew sharply (85% of core gamers negative in late 2025), while AI inside game systems, such as difficulty, drew the least objection. Regulation moved from guidance to dated duties: EU AI Act Article 50(1) since 2 August 2026, WCAG 2.1 AA for large US public-school entities from 26 April 2027, and EU consumer principles for bought in-game currency. Inference: Sonnet Man's strengths (no ads, no cloud AI, a real performer-educator, Shakespeare written into curricula on both sides of the Atlantic) line up with these trends. Inference: its risks are a long core loop, a model on the path to first play, a judge that can feel like a coin flip, and credits, a marketplace and referrals that sit badly with a classroom audience of children. The presentation and hip-hop-theme strand is the thinnest, because the shared search budget ran out.

## Ten findings most likely to change what Sonnet Man should do next

1. **Make the default duel fit a five-minute session.** Marvel Snap's designers target about three minutes a match and a top-quartile mobile session lasts about 5.2 minutes, so a three-round duel of full sonnets is the wrong default; short forms should be the quick match and the sonnet a long or ranked mode, with resumable matches. (Sections 1 and 4)
2. **Let the first verse happen before any model loads.** Poki saw conversion-to-play rise from 50% to 72% when median load fell from 29.5 s to 3.7 s, and it counts play from the first input, so the first duel should run on the rule-based judge and written opponent while the model downloads behind it. (Section 4)
3. **Show the code-checked craft score first, line by line, and keep the model's share bounded and explained.** Players accept scores that show why (NYT's Cross Bot, Semantle's proximity rank, Duolingo's Explain My Answer) and turned against luck that felt arbitrary and slow (Pokémon TCG Pocket's coin flips), so the verdict ceremony should also be short and skippable. (Sections 1, 2 and 4)
4. **Add a one-line AI notice at the first interaction now.** EU AI Act Article 50(1) has applied since 2 August 2026, the Digital Omnibus did not delay it, and the information is due "at the latest at the time of the first interaction" in an accessible form. (Section 3)
5. **Sell the player's verse and the real Devon Glover, and cast the AI only as sparring partner and referee.** Core gamers were 83% negative on AI-generated dialogue, Arc Raiders re-recorded AI voice lines after a 2/5 review, and hip-hop audiences rewarded real artists while doubting a hip-hop game with an AI-looking trailer, so recorded Glover performance and his recite-then-remix method are the assets to build on. (Sections 3 and 9)
6. **Keep credits, ranks and tournament results off model-only verdicts.** The most public 2025 case of players beating an AI judge (Where Winds Meet) used social tricks such as a parenthetical claim that the task was done, so wherever something of value is at stake the small on-device judge needs a rules gate plus a server-side or ensemble check. (Section 3)
7. **Treat school procurement as a product requirement.** US classroom adoption runs through Clever (60% of US students monthly) and Canvas LTI, districts now score privacy certification and accessibility, and large public-school entities must meet WCAG 2.1 AA from 26 April 2027, so classroom mode needs SSO and LTI, student accounts with credits, marketplace and referrals switched off, and a published conformance report. (Sections 6 and 8)
8. **Add one shared verse of the day with a plain-text, spoiler-free share and a forgiving streak.** The habit leaders run one short shared puzzle a day (NYT: 11.2B plays in 2025; LinkedIn: 84% next-day return), and Duolingo's one-off streak revival brought back 15.4M learners, in line with peer-reviewed evidence that repairable streaks soften the drop after a break. (Sections 2 and 5)
9. **Decide whether credits can be bought before building more on them.** Currency that can only be earned falls outside the EU CPC principles, while bought currency brings real-money pricing on every item, 14-day refunds of unused credits and spending switched off by default in any game not meant only for adults. (Section 7)
10. **Check forms with per-language prosody rules, not hyphenation, and say where text is machine-made.** Each verse tradition counts a different unit and hyphenation dictionaries such as Pyphen are not syllable counters, while players punish bad per-language text in public (Silksong's Chinese reviews, The Alters' undisclosed AI translation) and open test oracles exist for Arabic and Spanish. (Section 8)

### Cards create decisions when every option costs something

Developer statements from 2025-26 converge on three rules: no free "pass-through" options, randomness as a risk the player chooses, and no mechanic that dictates one way to play.

Mega Crit rebuilt Slay the Spire 2's events around cost. "This time around we're trying to avoid having any options where you simply do nothing and pass through", events "don't all have positive choices for the player", and "Don't just seek the unknown", because fighting is sometimes the better way to strengthen a deck. In the Slippery Bridge event the player must remove a card but can first "hold on", taking damage to re-randomise which card goes. There are over 50 events, varying by act and by the player's state, and Yano said there would be no alternative starting decks, only "more choices at the start of the run" (June 2025) ([Slay the Spire 2 events get an overhaul to force you into "difficult decisions"](https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/events-redesign), PCGamesN, 2025-06-13, citing Mega Crit's [Steam news post](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/646570/view/529848673510623660)) [V]. Hundreds of card ideas were cut in an "incredibly destructive process" ([It's kind of like you're a butcher…](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/card-games/its-kind-of-like-youre-a-butcher-hundreds-of-slay-the-spire-2-card-ideas-were-cut-during-development-in-an-incredibly-destructive-process), PC Gamer, 2026-03-06) [V, headline and subhead only]. An early idea to shrink the card pool was rejected by testers: "We need new stuff!" ([Slay the Spire 2 dev says an early idea was to actually reduce the card pool…](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/slay-the-spire-2-dev-says-an-early-idea-was-to-actually-reduce-the-card-pool-but-players-hated-it-we-need-new-stuff/), PC Gamer, 2026-04-23) [V, headline and subhead only]. ### Players forgave random outcomes more than random delays

The clearest randomness failure of the period was Pokémon TCG Pocket's coin flips. Players complained that flip animations drag battles out over about five screens; Misty had been "an offender since launch", a new Eevee card could chain flips, and Celebi EX with Serperior could produce 12 or more. One Reddit user said they stopped battling and open the app less. Players asked for speed-up or skip options, and no fix was reported (January 2025) (["Irritating" Pokemon TCG Pocket mechanic is turning players off the game](https://dexerto.com/gaming/irritating-pokemon-tcg-pocket-mechanic-is-turning-players-off-the-game-3020518), Dexerto, 2025-01-03) [V]. One player logged 299 flips (158 heads, 141 tails) and suspected a "hard mode"; others replied that shuffling and inconsistent energy mattered more ([Pokemon TCG Pocket player tracked coin flips…](https://www.sportskeeda.com/pokemon/pokemon-tcg-pocket-player-tracked-coin-flips-claim-game-was-on-hard-mode), Sportskeeda, 2025) [S]. Players also argued that going first was a disadvantage in that meta ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/pokemon/pokemon-tcg-pocket-players-discuss-disadvantages-going-first-current-meta), Sportskeeda, 2025) [S].

Inference: the complaint was as much about the time luck took as about luck itself. Mega Crit's "hold on" re-roll shows the alternative: variance as a resource the player chooses to spend. No 2025-26 talk or patch rationale on draw smoothing, mulligans, Discover-style choices or pity timers was found.

### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: match length is the largest structural risk.** Snap targets about 3 minutes and a top-quartile mobile session is about 5.2 minutes. Three rounds in which each move is a sonnet will run well past that. Couplet, haiku or quatrain rounds should be the default quick duel, with the sonnet as a long or ranked mode and matches that resume across sessions.
- **Inference: the model judge is Sonnet Man's coin flip.** Pocket's backlash centred on luck that felt arbitrary and slow to resolve. A judge that varies from run to run will read as luck unless the code-checked craft score is shown first and deterministically, the judge's share is capped and explained, and the verdict animation is short and skippable.
- **Inference: give a trailing player a stake and an exit.** Snap's doubling (and, unverified, its cheap retreat) and Mega Crit's paid re-roll turn risk into a choice. A raise-the-stakes move before round 3, or a "bow out" that loses less, keeps the trailing player deciding.
- **Inference: every card should cost something, and no device should dominate.** Mega Crit removed do-nothing options; Hearthstone found prescriptive mechanics homogenised its meta. A writing-device card should trade score against difficulty, not hand out a free bonus or push everyone toward one form.
- **Inference: content cadence matters more than the launch.** Shadowverse's first set (+786% daily revenue), Hearthstone's cards-every-patch plan and Mega Crit's "We need new stuff!" testers agree. Seasons should add forms, characters and relics on a fixed rhythm. Sixteen languages multiply that cost, which argues for devices that code can check in any language.
- **Inference: "free, no ads" is a review asset; say it plainly.** Shadowverse earned well but rated "Mostly Negative" over greed, while Mega Crit's "microtransaction haters" stance went with "Overwhelmingly Positive" reviews.
- **Inference: web portals will not bring this audience by genre.** Their card leaders are solitaire and UNO-style games. Classroom mode, tournaments, the Glover brand and his live circuit are the likelier channels, and the portals' "learn in seconds" lesson still applies to the first match.
- **Inference: co-op is an untested lever.** Sensor Tower credits Slay the Spire 2's four-player co-op in part. A co-written verse, built line by line by a team, fits the existing multiplayer and classroom modes.

### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: explain every verdict, Cross Bot style.** Show each craft check that passed or failed, the judge's one-line reason, and, after the round, "a stronger line you could have written". This is likely the cheapest gain in perceived fairness.
- **Inference: publish the judge's limits per language.** Semantle names its model's gaps. Small on-device models judging 16 languages will have per-language blind spots; a short "known limits" note per language, and showing the rules-only score beside the model's, would answer "the AI is wrong" before it spreads.
- **Inference: if players vote, plan for spite and cliques.** Direct rivals should not vote, or their votes should carry little weight, and voters and losers should earn a small reward, as Auto Rap Battles pays "rap bars" for wins, losses and voting. Strict vote rules belong where stakes are high (tournaments), as Dress to Impress did with its Master server.
- **Inference: on-device judging is not a hook in itself.** Jabby's local judge that explained its verdicts drew one review. The pull has to come from the duel and the brand; NYT's "human-crafted" line suggests the message "the verse is yours; the AI only referees".
- **Inference: the genre is open but demand is unproven.** Plan for niche-sized organic discovery and lean on channels that already reach poetry and Shakespeare audiences (sections 6 and 9).

### What players rejected: generative work that replaces a human's

Rejection hardened within a year. Quantic Foundry surveyed 1,799 gamers in October-December 2025: 85% were negative toward generative AI in games and 63% chose the most negative option; AI-generated dialogue drew 83% negative and quests 77%, against 46% for quests and dialogue in its 2024 survey; attitudes soften as uses move away from art and creative features, and dynamic difficulty adjustment was the most accepted use. The sample is skewed (74% core, 90% PC) ([Quantic Foundry: most gamers are negative about the use of Gen AI in video games](https://www.gamesmarket.global/quantic-foundry-most-gamers-are-negative-about-the-use-of-gen-ai-in-video-games), GamesMarket, 2025-12-22; the primary page returned 403) [V]. A secondary report puts difficulty adjustment at 26% positive and 24% neutral ([Tech4Gamers](https://tech4gamers.com/63-of-gamers-are-highly-negative-towards-generative-ai/), Dec 2025) [S]. Female and non-binary gamers were much more negative than male gamers, and older gamers more favourable (same GamesMarket source) [V].

Developers moved the same way. In GDC's 2026 survey of more than 2,300 respondents (published January 2026), 52% said generative AI harms the industry (30% in 2025, 18% in 2024) and 7% said it helps; 36% use it at work ([GDC 2026 State of the Game Industry…](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/), GDC, date not shown on page; the Business Wire release is dated 2026-01-29) [V]. 80.lv reported 55% and 40%, which conflicts with GDC's own figures; use GDC's ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-2026-survey-55-of-developers-consider-ai-s-impact-on-games-negative/), date not shown) [S].

Backlash reached reviews and awards. On Steam in 2026, games with at least 50 reviews averaged 77% positive when they disclosed AI and 83% when they did not, with no causation shown ([Almost half of Steam's October releases disclosed AI use](https://powerupgaming.co.uk/2026/11/02/almost-half-of-steams-october-releases-disclosed-ai-use/), Power Up Gaming, 2026-11-02) [V]. Text in the player's own verse that addresses the judge is the same attack.

### On-device AI was chosen for latency and cost; nobody published the bill

Publishers that shipped player-facing AI at scale on PC chose on-device models on NVIDIA RTX GPUs and cited latency ("extremely low", Krafton, 2025-10-30) [V]; the price was reach, since Smart Zoi is RTX-only and "experimental" (NVIDIA, 2025-03-13) [V]. A third-party wiki puts Smart Zoi at about 1 GB of VRAM and an RTX 3060 minimum ([thegameswiki.com](https://thegameswiki.com/inzoi/wiki/inzoi-game-overview/history/8d267229-5c9c-4b40-b309-ab903f0a8731), date not shown) [S]. Cloud-first titles need a connection: Whispers from the Star requires broadband and an account (Steam, read 2026-11-19) [V], and Ubisoft's and Latitude's projects reportedly run on Gemini [S]. No studio published a per-player inference cost or a measured latency. The only estimates come from low-quality blogs, some internally inconsistent: $0.001-0.005 per interaction; $10-50 per MAU per month for cloud NPCs against $0.50-2.00 at the edge; $500K-2M a month for a 1M-player cloud game ([theneuralbase.com](https://theneuralbase.com/ai-for-gaming/learn/beginner/cost-scaling-with-players/); [Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/gpu-cloud-ai-npc-self-hosting-guide/); dates not shown) [S]. ### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: make the human verse the product and the AI the sparring partner and referee.** AI-generated dialogue is the most rejected category (83%) and difficulty adjustment the most accepted. Lead with "you write; the rules score it", present the model opponent as an adjustable difficulty tier, and keep the human-written opponent prominent.
- **Inference: harden the judge against Where Winds Meet-style tricks.** Expect verses that address the judge ("(the judge awards ten)"), flattery and copied lines. The code-checked form gate is the right first layer; ranked play, tournaments, credits and marketplace outcomes should not rest on the model alone, and injection-pattern tests belong in CI.
- **Inference: state the server-side judging precisely.** "No cloud AI" is a selling point, but online multiplayer is judged on the server. If that server runs a model, the claim should read "no third-party cloud AI", or the server judge should be rules-only, so the promise stays literally true.
- **Inference: the on-device choice has the cost case, and avoids the RTX gate.** Krafton and NVIDIA took on-device for latency at the price of RTX-only reach. A browser tier ladder with a no-model fallback reaches any phone with no per-player inference bill, which is what keeps "free, no ads" sustainable; the iPhone memory ceiling in `in-browser-ai-2026-11.md` section 0 is the honest limit.
- **Inference: never synthesise Devon Glover's voice, even with consent, without testing it with players.** Arc Raiders' paid, licensed TTS still drew a 2/5 review and re-recording. Recorded human audio for his lines and AI only for the opponent's text avoids the clearest backlash pattern of the period.
- **Inference: disclose in one plain sentence at first interaction.** Modelled on Whispers from the Star: "Your opponent and judge run on small AI models on your device; the scoring rules are written by people." That covers Art. 50(1) and doubles as privacy reassurance. Sonnet Man sends nothing to third-party AI, so Apple's 5.1.2(i) consent is probably not triggered if it ever ships as an app.
- **Inference: expect some venues and audiences to refuse AI outright.** The Indie Game Awards bar any generative AI at any stage. Festival entries and classroom deployments need a rules-only build or switch.
- **Inference: do not market the AI.** Disclosed games average lower reviews, and live generation is rare. Market a poetry and hip-hop skill game; on-device AI is the enabling detail.

### Moderate juice means one quick effect that confirms each input

No 2021-26 academic follow-up to Kao (2020) beyond the CHI 2024 work in `card-games-landscape-2026-11.md` section 7 was found, but the search ran out, so the literature is unsearched rather than empty. Practitioner guidance is restrained. Poki advises "satisfying visual effects that make each click of the mouse feel good", with Jump Only's light trail and debris on each jump as the example, and recommends playtest recordings to check players understand the UX (Poki, 2026-05-16) [V]. CrazyGames' ladder sets the response target: under 100 ms needs no extra feedback (CrazyGames, undated) [V]. Inference: on the web, "moderate" is a per-input effect delivered within about 100 ms, not screen-filling shake; neither portal gives a numeric norm. Section 9 adds the contrast between Balatro's burning score, which rewards an outcome, and Pokémon TCG Pocket's flip chains, which block the next input.

Haptics cannot carry feedback on the mobile web. MDN says the Vibration API "does nothing if the device doesn't support it" and "is not Baseline because it does not work in some of the most widely-used browsers" ([Vibration API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Vibration_API), MDN, last modified 2024-04-11) [V]. That Safari on iOS has no `navigator.vibrate` is [U]; the project's own record of an uncaught `vibrate` call freezing games on iPhone is consistent with it but is an internal record, not a web source. An iOS 18 checkbox-switch haptic workaround is [U] and fragile. That `prefers-reduced-motion` reflects the OS setting in Safari is [U].

### Feedback on creative input is accepted when it names the rule and stays optional

The one primary 2026 source found is Duolingo's. Explain My Answer became free for most learners on 2026-01-01 in Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Portuguese, Italian and Korean courses. It "crafts personalized feedback that takes into account the error you made", tailors explanations "for learners who don't have grammatical gender rules in their language", works after correct answers too, shows the learner's answer beside the correct form with the rule and contrasting examples, and lets learners "choose whether you want feedback on a mistake". The post gives no usage, accuracy or satisfaction figures and mentions no human review ([Explain My Answer is now free](https://blog.duolingo.com/explain-my-answer-now-free), Duolingo, 2026-01-01) [V]. A November 2025 preprint on adaptive difficulty reports that "players appreciate transparency and a sense of control, and hidden adjustments can reduce trust" ([preprints.org 202511.2251](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202511.2251/v1/download), Preprints.org, Nov 2025; not peer reviewed; fetch returned 403) [S]. Inference: Duolingo's pattern maps directly onto a code-checked craft score: name the specific rule, show the player's line beside what the form required, offer it after a success too so it does not feel like punishment, and let the player open it.

### Difficulty and matchmaking: skill matching helps most players, and hidden bots breed distrust

Activision's 2024 white paper is the clearest public case for skill matching. In a North American test that loosened skill constraints for half a treatment group, only the top ~10% saw a meaningful fall in quit rates, judged temporary, "at the cost of much greater impact to the much larger 30 percent of the population toward the bottom of the skill distribution"; Activision says skill matching gives "80% to 90% of players" better results, longer play and fewer quits, and weights connection quality most of all factors ([Activision unveils in-depth white paper on Call of Duty's matchmaking system](https://gaminghq.eu/2024/07/30/activision-unveils-in-depth-white-paper-on-call-of-dutys-matchmaking-system/), GamingHQ, 2024-07-30; [esports.gg](https://cache.esports.gg/news/call-of-duty/skill-based-matchmaking-is-good-for-call-of-duty), 2024) [S; the primary PDF timed out]. ### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: the model download must never gate the first verse.** Poki's 50% to 72% conversion gain and its first-input rule mean the first duel should start with the rule-based judge and written opponent while the model streams in, following CrazyGames' "load tutorial assets first" pattern.
- **Inference: teach forms by writing one line, not by reading rules.** A text game is already text-heavy, so the first move should be a scaffolded fill-in with instant code-checked feedback ("finish this couplet; it must rhyme with *night*"), not an explanation of iambic pentameter. The Glover intro must be skippable, as Poki requires and as the owner's own "presented, skippable intro" rule says.
- **Inference: set realistic retention targets.** A D1 above 30% or a D7 of 6-7% would be top quartile; the best US Android casual genres (Sports, Racing, Tabletop) reach D30 of 8-11%, and Tabletop is the nearest genre proxy. - **Inference: make the craft score explainable the Duolingo way, and keep it visibly separate from the judge.** Line-level reasons ("line 3: 11 syllables; the stress breaks at *beautiful*") let a player who disputes the model's taste still see that the rules half was fair. No trust numbers were found, so test with players.
- **Inference: label AI opponents plainly and never disguise a bot as a human online.** If online multiplayer ever fills empty queues with a model, say so on the match card and rank AI wins separately.
- **Inference: a small player pool needs labelled-AI or asynchronous matches and rating uncertainty, not strict skill matching.** Activision's data supports skill matching, but a poetry game will not have that queue depth. Glicko-style deviation (unverified for Lichess) suits infrequent players; Clash Royale's symmetric steps and soft resets keep seasons fresh.
- **Inference: if the opponent adapts, say so, and never secretly soften the judge.** Named tiers (an "apprentice" allowed a set rate of metre slips) fit a game sold on fair judging better than silent adjustment.
- **Inference: feedback must work without haptics and must respect reduced motion.** Visual and audio first, within 100 ms, one effect per accepted line (a small burst when a rhyme lands), and every `vibrate` call guarded.

### The Wordle grid is still the model share, but nobody has new numbers on it

The spoiler-free emoji grid began when an early player in New Zealand typed the coloured squares by hand; Josh Wardle then built it in, and it spread through a group chat in late 2021 ([You're Playing Wordle Because A Nerd Group Chat In New Zealand Made It Viral](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/stefficao/how-wordle-went-viral-strategy), BuzzFeed News, 2022) [S]. Wordle went from about 90 daily users (November 2021) to about 300,000 by about 1 January 2022, and NYT later said it brought "tens of millions" of new users ([Wordle brought 'tens of millions' of new users to the New York Times](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/04/wordle-new-york-times-user-growth), TechCrunch, 2022-05-04) [S]. Twitter's own 2022 count was about 1.7M tweets mentioning Wordle; later in 2022 volume fell to about 200,000 a day ([The Wordle phenomenon](https://sentione.com/blog/the-wordle-phenomenon), SentiOne, date not shown) [S]. No company published 2025-26 data on the share of new players arriving through shared results.

Inference: the grid worked because it was spoiler-free, plain text that pastes into any chat, and about a puzzle everyone faced that day. Public score-posting fades as a novelty; sharing inside group chats, which nobody can measure, likely persists. NYT's annual recap moves sharing toward a yearly identity artefact.

### Asynchronous play is how small games give everyone an opponent

NYT's Crossplay (21 Jan 2026) is a free two-player app with seven hidden tiles from a finite bag, in-game chat (a first for NYT Games), play against the computer at several difficulty levels, and "smart matchmaking" by skill; launch promotion had Grammy-nominated artists Leslie Odom Jr., BRELAND and Amy Allen "challenging fans", with a submission contest from 21 Jan to 15 Feb 2026 (Editor & Publisher, 2026-01-21) [V]. Players can run several games at once ([Arcadia Quill](https://arcadiaquill.com/30880/features/introducing-the-new-york-times-newest-game-crossplay/), date not shown) [S]; Editor & Publisher says "real-time competition", which conflicts with turn-based play across simultaneous games.

Backpack Battles sells asynchronous PvP: you "face the builds of other players"; Unranked is "Low pressure without timers"; Ranked lets you "gain points against equal opponents, but still at your own pace"; Lobbies let you "match friends or streamers live with a timer!" (Steam page, read 2026-11-19) [V].

Platform channels for web games: Discord opened Activities to all developers in September 2024, reporting 200M monthly users (over 90% playing games) and apps "used by over 25% of our monthly active users" ([Build where the world plays](https://discord.com/blog/build-where-the-world-plays), Discord Blog, 2024-09-26) [V]; 2025-26 figures could not be confirmed from Discord. Reddit's 2025 Developer Funds paid by Daily Qualified Engagers (rolling 7-day average), from $500 at 500 to $75,000 at 1,000,000, up to $116,500 per app, plus $1,000 at 250 retained installs; Reddit later advertised "up to $500,000" and "up to $167,000 per app", and the ceilings conflict ([The continuation of Reddit Developer Funds](https://libreddit.winscloud.net/r/Devvit/comments/1jlhi8b/the_continuation_of_reddit_developer_funds), r/Devvit via mirror, 2025-03-27) [S]. Reddit ran a Daily Games Hackathon in January-February 2026 that valued repeatable return loops, and a Reddit page says most Redditors play its games on phones ([allhackathons](https://allhackathons.com/hackathon/the-reddit-daily-games-hackathon), 2026) [S].

Inference: the 2026 mainstream pattern for a turn-based word duel is computer play, skill matching, in-match chat and post-game analysis; indie PvP values "your own pace" against recorded builds, with live timed play kept for friends and streamers. Reddit pays for daily engagement, which makes a daily loop a direct revenue lever there.

### Clips are now flagged by the platform; creator economies pay a few creators well

Steam's Timeline API lets a game mark phases and add events, each with an icon, title, description and a clip priority of `Standard` or `Featured` ([Steam Timeline](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/timeline), Steamworks documentation, Valve, undated) [V]. Roblox said it paid over $1B to creators through DevEx in 2025 against $923M in 2024, and the median of more than 29,000 DevEx creators received $1,440 in the 12 months to 2025-06-30 ([Roblox Q3 2025 earnings call](https://www.alphaspread.com/security/nyse/rblx/investor-relations/earnings-call/q3-2025), 2025; [Roblox 2025 Economic Impact Report](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250905587466/en), Business Wire, 2025-09-05; fetch refused) [S]. Inference: a web game has no platform clip pipeline, so it must make its own share card or clip.

No data was gathered on spread through classrooms (Blooket, Gimkit, Teachers Pay Teachers, teacher TikTok), TikTok or Shorts tie-ins, tournaments in small games, or modding. Referral programmes are effectively unanswered: no sourced data on effectiveness, abuse or rules for minors was found. COPPA is covered as [S] and [U] in `card-games-landscape-2026-11.md` section 5 and `in-browser-ai-2026-11.md` section 7.

### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: add one shared "verse of the day".** One short prompt and one form, the same for everyone, creates the common ritual that makes results comparable, as NYT and LinkedIn do. It also suits Reddit's pay-for-daily-engagement model.
- **Inference: share the verse as a spoiler-free text artefact.** A paste-able card carrying the form, the round results, a short excerpt and a link works in any chat, and the verse itself is shareable content a coloured grid never had. A yearly "Year in Verse", after NYT's recap, extends it.
- **Inference: make streaks forgiving and never punish absence.** Freezes, repair and occasional revival follow the JCR finding and Duolingo's 15.4M revivals; punishing non-return would also push the classroom game into PEGI's stricter band.
- **Inference: fill online multiplayer with asynchronous verse "ghosts".** A server-judged duel against another player's recorded verses gives a human opponent without a wait, as Backpack Battles does with builds; keep live matches for friends, classes and tournaments.
- **Inference: a free post-duel review sets the game apart.** NYT sells Cross Bot to subscribers. A free critique of metre, rhyme and form from the code checks and the on-device model fits a no-ads game and is what a classroom wants.
- **Inference: Discord Activities and Reddit are reachable channels, with a technical risk.** Whether on-device models run inside their embedded frames was not checked and needs a test.
- **Inference: a recurring "beat the Sonnet Man" challenge has a direct precedent.** Crossplay launched with musicians challenging fans and a three-week contest; Glover can publish a daily prompt or a ghost verse.
- **Inference: keep referrals and the marketplace low-stakes and recognition-based.** With no referral evidence, a $1,440 median Roblox creator income and minors in scope, featuring players' verses in the theatre and gallery is a safer motivator than credits for recruits, especially for under-13s.

### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: rostering is the first wall.** Passkey sign-in suits adults but does not roster a class. A Clever, ClassLink and Google SSO path and an LTI 1.3 launch likely decide whether districts can adopt classroom mode at all. This refines `card-games-landscape-2026-11.md` Implications 12: keep a no-account play path for one-off lessons, but procurement needs SSO and LTI.
- **Inference: commerce features clash with a student mode.** Credits, a marketplace and referrals are what privacy reviews look at, and only 45% of top tools are certified. Student accounts should have them off, stated in a data-privacy agreement and a certification bid (iKeepSafe or 1EdTech).
- **Inference: on-device AI is a real but unrecognised advantage.** No pupil text leaves the device, which matches DfE's bar on training with pupil work. Server-judged multiplayer weakens the claim, so classroom play should default to on-device or rules-only judging, and the privacy documents have to make the argument because no procurement checklist rewards it.
- **Inference: publish a standards map and play-specific decks.** RL.9-10.9 maps to remix rounds, RL.x.4 to device cards, RL.11-12.7 to performance, and the GCSE anthology (15+ poems) to fixed revision targets. Teachers who "rely on texts they have taught for a long time" need decks for the plays they already teach, once set lists are verified.
- **Inference: evidence now counts.** Over half the 2026 top 40 have ESSA Level IV evidence. A Level IV logic model now, and a small classroom study with a partner such as an RSC Associate School or a Poetry Out Loud state agency later, is the realistic path.
- **Inference: build a teacher-hosted live format.** A projected "class versus the Sonnet Man" round or a class bracket in 10-minute blocks fits the Kahoot!-shaped habit better than one-to-one phone duels.
- **Inference: let teachers see and override the judge.** Teachers rate AI weakest for grading and feedback, and two-thirds of students worry about critical thinking. Rule checks shown line by line, plus a teacher override, address both.
- **Inference: time a recitation tie-in to Poetry Out Loud's season.** Its August to mid-March window and 2-3 week classroom unit leave room for a spoken-verse practice mode, and Glover's school circuit is the route in.

### Free web games earn from portal ads or direct payments; ad-free models are rare

Poki's preferred deal is web-exclusive for five years by default across "the browser/open web, Discord, and YouTube Playables" (not Steam, mobile stores or consoles); Poki pays a revenue share on players it brings, and "Everything a player generates when they come to your game directly… is yours"; the non-exclusive alternative is "a one-time flat license fee"; no percentages are published and terms are "indicative" ([Revenue & deal types](https://developers.poki.com/guide/revenue-deal-types), Poki for Developers, read 2026-11-19) [V]. A third-party claim of 100% on own traffic and 50/50 on Poki's is unconfirmed ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-poki), 2026) [S]. CrazyGames pays through "advertising revenue share and optional in-game purchases", monthly above €100; games must carry no external ads and must integrate its SDK; it is non-exclusive, "a complementary channel, not a conflicting one" ([FAQ](https://docs.crazygames.com/faq/), CrazyGames, read 2026-11-19) [V]. A 2026 guide found no published split and cites jam terms of 60% of ad revenue and 70% of purchases to the developer, NET 60 payment and invite-only purchases through Xsolla ([CrazyGames Developer Guide: Publish and Earn (2026)](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-crazygames), Cinevva, 2026-07-17) [V].

Web payments got cheaper after Epic v. Apple. ### Regulation: the EU's currency principles are the most specific new text

The CPC Network's "Key principles on in-game virtual currencies" (21 Mar 2025) cover currencies bought with real money and exclude those that "can solely be obtained through gameplay". They are non-binding and following them is "no guarantee for compliance" ([Key Principles on In-game Virtual Currencies](https://esportslegal.news/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies.pdf), CPC Network / European Commission, Ref. Ares(2025)2303808, 2025-03-21, read via a mirror) [V]:

| Principle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Real-money prices "clearly and prominently displayed" for currency and items, without quantity discounts in the item price |
| 2 | Avoid "mixing different in-game virtual currencies in one video game" and multi-step exchanges |
| 3 | Avoid selling currency only in bundles that mismatch item prices |
| 4 | Pre-purchase information: item, trader identity, real price, withdrawal right, guarantee, payment, delivery |
| 5 | Withdrawal within 14 days from buying unused currency, which "is not digital content"; losing the right needs a separate express click |
| 6 | Unfair terms include unilateral changes to currency value, removing bought content, and bans with no way to contest |
| 7 | "any video game which is not exclusively intended for an adult audience should expect a significant part of its player base to be under the age of 18"; do not "directly exhort children to buy"; spending disabled by default unless adult-only; whale-targeting judged at "a stricter threshold" |

### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: decide whether credits can be bought before anything else.** Earned-only credits sit outside the CPC principles. If any route sells them, even a "supporter pack", Sonnet Man must show real-money prices on every credit-priced item, avoid a second currency, sell exact amounts, refund unused credits within 14 days and switch spending off by default, because a classroom game is plainly not adult-only.
- **Inference: the marketplace and referrals are the riskiest features for children.** Principle 7 forbids exhorting children to buy; a credits-for-invites reward works much like that. Roblox's 2026 tiers show the direction. Marketplace trading and referral rewards should be off by default below 16 and in classroom accounts.
- **Inference: follow Marvel Rivals and Guild Wars 2 on seasons, not Fortnite.** Season rewards that never expire, a way to claim missed ones, no mid-season length changes, and countdowns that are true, since fake urgency timers were among the CMA's first targets.
- **Inference: never sell random packs.** Pocket's year two fell 68% and depended on new sets, and Korea fines any gap between stated and actual odds. Fixed or earned collection rewards remove the whole question.
- **Inference: use portals for reach, not revenue.** Both pay from SDK-served ads; Poki's five-year exclusivity also covers Discord and YouTube Playables, which would block the Discord channel in section 5. A non-exclusive CrazyGames-style listing that links back keeps "no ads" intact.
- **Inference: build revenue on a small group of recurring supporters.** Xsolla's repeat-buyer share and Lichess's record month point the same way; Lichess's US pause shows the legal entity and payment set-up come before the donate button.
- **Inference: any subscription needs easy cancellation even though the FTC rule died.** State laws and the CMA still apply. An invoiced annual school licence probably avoids most consumer-subscription rules, but school-licence research was not possible.
- **Inference: the revenue plan has research holes.** Performer-brand merchandise and live events, education grants and school licence prices are the sources most specific to this game, and none was researched.

### The law reaches games through schools, shops and chat

Under Ian Hamilton's layman's reading (not legal advice), the European Accessibility Act has applied since 28 June 2025; games "generally" fall outside it as products, but player chat counts as electronic communications ("If you have voice chat, you must also provide text chat"), microtransactions count as e-commerce, web games and sites must meet EN 301 549, and microenterprises (under 10 staff and turnover or balance sheet of €2m or less) are exempt ([Demystifying EAA & GPSR](https://igda-gasig.org/what-and-why/demystifying-eaa-gpsr/), IGDA Game Accessibility SIG, 2025-06) [V]. Taylor Wessing reads it the same way ([Accessibility in the gaming industry](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/03/accessibility-in-the-gaming-industry), 2025-03-24) [S].

The DOJ's fact sheet sets WCAG 2.1 Level AA for ADA Title II; the 20 April 2026 interim rule moved compliance to 26 April 2027 (50,000+ people) and 26 April 2028 (smaller entities and special districts); public schools, community colleges and universities are covered; a school district takes the population of its city or county; government apps must comply "even if the app is run by a private company", and vendor content and tools are not excepted ([Fact Sheet: New Rule on the Accessibility of Web Content and Mobile Apps…](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/), ADA.gov, DOJ, page dated 2024-04-08 and referencing the 2026 rule) [V]. The CVAA covers in-game voice and text chat; the game-software waiver ended at the close of 2018, and new software from 1 January 2019 must remove barriers where "achievable" ([Communications Accessibility in Games](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/client-advisories/communications-accessibility-in-games-what-game-developers-need-to-know.md), Kelley Drye, 2018-03-20) [V]. A claim that the waiver lapsed in 2024 conflicts with this and is treated as unreliable ([disabilityworld.org](https://www.disabilityworld.org/articles/game-accessibility-2026/)) [U].

### Text- and speech-heavy play: scale text, read aloud, keep typing equal to speaking

"Dyslexia fonts" do not help. A 2026 meta-analysis of 15 studies (91 effect sizes, N = 688) comparing OpenDyslexic and Dyslexie with Times New Roman and Arial found "no consistent or reliable effect" (g = −0.04, 95% CI −0.15 to 0.07, p = 0.5) ([Does font improve reading in dyslexic children? Meta-analysis…](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/97732/), *Annals of Dyslexia* 76, Strathprints, University of Strathclyde, 2026) [V]. Apple's Accessibility Reader (announced May 2025) offers font, colour and spacing options and read-aloud for "dyslexia or low vision"; Personal Voice now needs "only 10 recorded phrases" but is limited to the user's own voice "for their own personal, noncommercial use" ([Apple unveils powerful accessibility features coming later this year](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/05/apple-unveils-powerful-accessibility-features-coming-later-this-year/), Apple Newsroom, 2025-05-13) [V].

Speech recognition for atypical speech is improving but closed. Google's Project Euphonia expanded to Spanish, Japanese, French and Hindi, held "over 1.5 million utterances from ~3,000 speakers" as of February 2025, keeps the corpus proprietary and offers open-source curation tools ([Project Euphonia: Advancing Inclusive Speech Recognition…](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/flang.2025.1569448/full), *Frontiers in Language Sciences*, 2025-06-20) [S]. For the browser speech stack (Web Speech API sends audio to a server by default; Whisper is uneven across languages), see `in-browser-ai-2026-11.md` section 4. XAG 116 covers time limits [S]; WCAG 2.2.1's turn-off, adjust or extend options are [U]. Apple defines captions as "time-synchronized text" for "the dialog and relevant sounds" (Apple ANL page) [V].

Canvas and WebGL games need a hand-built semantic mirror. PhET's Parallel DOM mirrors the scene graph as hidden HTML and routes alerts through one polite live region ([Parallel DOM Architecture… (review)](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69bcaadb67dbed0dcc79a4bf), a11y-paradise, date not shown) [S]; a conference talk found `aria-live="assertive"` less reliable than moving focus for urgent announcements ([a11y & Interactive Canvases](https://gitnation.com/contents/a11y-and-interactive-canvases), GitNation, date not shown) [S]; Phaser has no built-in solution ([Phaser issue 7294 mirror](https://google.18789066745.workers.dev/phaserjs/phaser/issues/7294)) [S].

### Each verse tradition counts a different unit, and hyphenation is not scansion

Open tools exist for two of Sonnet Man's harder languages. ### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: schools make ADA Title II the real compliance driver.** Vendor tools are not excepted and large public entities face WCAG 2.1 AA from 26 April 2027, so districts will ask for a conformance report within a year. The 3D theatre and VR gallery are the likely failures unless they have a DOM equivalent.
- **Inference: native screen-reader play is an open lane.** A verse duel is mostly text and state. A DOM-first board with one polite live region and focus moves for urgent events could be fully playable with VoiceOver, TalkBack and NVDA, meeting Apple's "all common tasks" bar, which includes the passkey sign-in.
- **Inference: verse read-aloud must share the audio channel with the screen reader.** Blizzard's own diagnosis applies to voiced verse, beats and the theatre: separate volume per channel, ducking under speech and captions for every voiced line.
- **Inference: speech-as-a-move needs an equal typed path, an editable transcript and adjustable timing.** Small on-device recognisers cannot use Euphonia's data, so the judge must score the verse, not the recogniser's errors, and round timers should be adjustable or removable.
- **Inference: do not headline a "dyslexia font".** Offer 200% text scaling, adjustable spacing, high contrast and read-aloud of card text, the set Apple's Accessibility Reader ships.
- **Inference: the 16-language form checker is a fairness risk as well as a feature.** If it counts syllables from hyphenation breakpoints it will miscount, worst across word boundaries (Spanish synalepha, French elision). Use per-language counters, test against Qawafi and Rantanplan, and disclose any machine translation of card text or judge feedback with a way to report bad strings.
- **Inference: RTL and CJK must fit a mobile web budget.** Arabic needs a mirrored UI; CJK needs sliced fonts, since a raw CJK weight is far too large for a phone; verse drawn in WebGL needs full shaping, so compositing DOM text into the scene is safer.
- **Inference: the marketplace may bring in the EAA.** With no chat, the EAA and CVAA matter little, but purchasable credits or a shop count as e-commerce under the EAA unless the operator is a microenterprise, and adding any chat would bring in both laws. Not legal advice.

In Early Access, Yano kept placeholder art on purpose: "I wanted to set the precedent that some of the stuff is incomplete, and I wanted to make it obvious" ([Mega Crit explains why Slay the Spire 2 uses garbage placeholder art](https://ingamenews.com/pc-gaming/mega-crit-explains-why-slay-the-spire-2-uses-garbage-placeholder-art/), InGameNews, 2026) [S]; of the ending, "I do want it to be more extravagant when you win" ([What will Slay the Spire 2's final ending be like?](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/games/card-games/slay-the-spire-2-ending/), PC Gamer, 2026) [S]. Shadowverse's revenue-spiking set included "two animated cards", but their separate effect is not isolated (PocketGamer.biz, 2025-07-28) [V]. Pocket's coin-flip, pack-opening and Wonder Pick sequences were all named as slow, and one player opened the app less because of them (Dexerto, 2025-01-03) [V]. Marvel Snap's team tests on real devices because "touch quirks and vibration feedback" do not show in the editor (Out of Games, about mid-2023) [V].

No 2025-26 data linking art or audio quality to retention or spending was found, and Pocket's immersive cards, Snap's 3D card effects and Hearthstone's announcers could not be verified. Inference: juice that rewards an outcome (a burning score) reads as feel, and juice that blocks the next input (flip chains) reads as friction tied to lower play. Slay the Spire 2 shipped deliberately rough art and still sold an estimated 7M units, so art polish did not gate traction for a design-led card game. For a phone-friendly "card comes alive" moment (QR plus camera tracking), see `xr-card-games-2026-11.md` section 8.

### Voice: human performance for the brand, consent rules for any replica

Arc Raiders' AI-voice reversal is covered in section 3. The 2025 SAG-AFTRA Interactive Media Agreement (term 8 Nov 2022 to 31 Oct 2028) sets the industry norm: digital-replica consent must be "clear and conspicuous" in a separate document or signed rider, with a "reasonably specific description" of use that says whether real-time generation is involved; consent generally cannot be taken at hiring for future games; vocal replica pay is per generated line (about 10 words); a usage report is due "within 90 days of a game's release" ([Inside the New SAG-AFTRA Interactive Media Agreement…](https://fkks.com/?passle-post=102mewu), Frankfurt Kurnit Klein + Selz, 2026-01-29) [V]. Members ratified it in July 2025 with 95.04% in favour (announcement dated 9 or 24 July depending on source) ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/sag-aftra-ratify-interactive-media-agreement-video-game-strike/), 2025-07) [S]. Apple's Personal Voice cannot serve here, since it is licensed for the user's own "personal, noncommercial use" (Apple Newsroom, 2025-05-13) [V]. For open text-to-speech models and their language coverage, see `in-browser-ai-2026-11.md` section 4.

### Devon Glover is a working performer-educator with a 2026 calendar

The NEH profile describes Glover as a Brooklyn-raised African American rapper and Shakespeare ambassador trained as a math educator, for whom the 2001 film *O* showed that Shakespeare "was meant to be heard and seen". His method is to recite Shakespeare's original lines, then turn them into hip-hop with his own lyrics, spoken and sometimes improvised (the example is Sonnet 18). He created content for Flocabulary, has toured the US, Europe and Africa, and says "What makes me more joyous than anything is when I get a response from a student who has begun writing"; the article mentions no books or albums (NEH, Spring 2023) [V]. His 2026 calendar, from schedules and listings (none of the sources confirms the events took place as planned): the Shakespeare Association of America's annual meeting (1-4 Apr 2026) listed him to co-lead the workshop "From Bard to Beat: Teaching Shakespeare Through Hip-Hop and Poetry" with Katherine Steele Brokaw on 2 April and to perform "Sonnets and Soliloquies: An Evening with The Sonnet Man" with violinist Leoncarlo Canlas on 3 April, with audience participation in dancing and singing; the programme calls him "rapper, poet, teacher, playwright, director, and actor" ([SAA 2026 Schedule](https://shakespeareassociation.org/annual-meetings/2026-schedule-2/), Shakespeare Association of America) [V]. ### Against Sonnet Man

- **Inference: spend presentation effort on the verdict moment, and keep it short.** Balatro's burning score rewards an outcome; Pocket's flip chains block input. A line that lands deserves one strong effect; the verdict sequence must be skippable and never stand between the player and the next round.
- **Inference: art polish is not the gate.** Slay the Spire 2 sold an estimated 7M units on deliberately rough art. Animated character cards are better spent as part of season content drops, as Shadowverse bundled them.
- **Inference: use the real Devon Glover, visibly credited and paid.** Recorded performances, his own remixes as model answers after a round, real beats and his live violin collaborations protect the brand; AI likenesses or a synthetic Glover voice put it at risk, and any replica would need SAG-AFTRA-style written, use-specific consent naming real-time generation, plus a usage record and per-line pay.
- **Inference: turn his signature act into a mechanic.** A "recite-then-remix" round, where the card gives the original line and the player writes the remix, matches his method and the RL.9-10.9 standard in section 6.
- **Inference: avoid the OnlyHands pattern.** Show real gameplay in every trailer, use no AI-looking marketing, and do not imply artist involvement that does not exist.
- **Inference: take care with gestures and slang in a children's game.** The Snoop emote criticism shows that hip-hop iconography can read as gang signalling to parents and schools; classroom content needs a review pass.
- **Inference: make Glover's calendar the live-ops calendar.** Festival and conference dates (SAA in April, Stratford in April, summer festivals) are natural moments for a "beat the Sonnet Man" challenge, following Crossplay's musician-led launch.
