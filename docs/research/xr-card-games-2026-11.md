# AR, VR, XR and hybrid card games: landscape study for Sonnet Man

Date of research: 2026-11-16. Scope: XR card games, physical-digital hybrids, web platform state, interaction lessons, splats, co-located play, business reality. General card-game design and in-browser AI models are out of scope (covered elsewhere).

## How to read this report

**Evidence labels**

- **[F]** the page was opened and the fact read there.
- **[S]** the fact comes from a search-result summary of that URL; the page was not opened.
- **UNVERIFIED** no source found, or not searched.
- **Inference** my reasoning from cited facts, not a sourced claim.

**Coverage limit.** The session's web-search budget (200 searches, shared with two research sub-agents) ran out. Platform facts in section 3 were then checked by opening known primary pages directly, so they are the strongest part. The catalogue (sections 1-2) and the business section (7) have gaps, listed in section 9. Nothing below is from memory unless it says so.

**Bottom line.** The web can deliver the "card comes alive" moment on an ordinary phone today, but not through WebXR on iPhone. Headset XR is real on Quest and Android XR, VR-only on Vision Pro, and the audience there is small and shrinking. Build phone-first camera AR and treat headsets as an optional mode.

The failures made the digital service the point of the product and then could not fund it.

### 3.2 Apple, stated plainly

**What does NOT work in iPhone or iPad Safari (or any iOS browser, since all use WebKit):**

- No WebXR of any kind: no `navigator.xr` sessions, no `immersive-ar`, no hit test, no anchors, no hand input, no WebXR image tracking. caniuse lists Safari on iOS as unsupported through 27.2. [F]
- No way for a web page to get ARKit world tracking or to draw live 3D registered to the room inside the page.

**What does work on iPhone Safari:**

- **Camera access through `getUserMedia`**, so JavaScript computer-vision libraries can track an image in the camera feed and draw over it (section 3.3). Zappar's docs list Safari on iOS 11.3+ as "best" supported. [F] https://docs.zap.works/universal-ar/javascript/getting-started/compatibility/
- **AR Quick Look:** a link to a USDZ or `.reality` file opens Apple's own AR viewer, outside the page. It supports animation, audio and behaviours in `.reality` files. [F] https://developer.apple.com/augmented-reality/quick-look/ Apple's WWDC19 session says Quick Look supports image anchors, where content "can track and follow a real-world image". [F] https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/612/ Whether image-anchored files still behave this way on iOS 27 was not tested; treat as a promising lead, not a fact.
- **The `<model>` element:** in Safari 27.0 (17 Sep 2026) it reached iOS, iPadOS and macOS, accepting USDZ and `.glb`, with a fallback image and a path into Quick Look. [F] https://webkit.org/blog/18325/webkit-features-in-safari-27-0/ It shows a 3D model in the page; it is not AR by itself.
- **WebGPU** since Safari 26 (Sep 2025). [S] https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/
- **App Clips as a bridge:** Needle's "Needle Go" App Clip implements the WebXR API on ARKit; a link shows an App Clip card, the user taps, and the same WebXR page runs with hit test, plane detection, DOM overlay and image tracking (anchors not yet). It works for any WebXR URL, carries Needle branding unless paid, and is a third party's App Store asset, so it is an app-store dependency by another name. [F] https://engine.needle.tools/docs/how-to-guides/xr/ios-webxr-app-clip.md

**Vision Pro:** WebXR is VR-only. In June 2026 an Apple engineer wrote that `immersive-ar` "is not currently supported on visionOS" and pointed to Quick Look, the model element and immersive environments instead; a user reported the experimental AR flag was removed in visionOS 27 (user report only). [F] https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/826845 So on Vision Pro a web card game is either a flat Safari window, a fully virtual VR scene, or inline 3D models.

## 4. Interaction design lessons for cards in XR

Evidence here is thinner than for platforms: two Meta documents, one Apple session and shipped-game behaviour. Items marked Inference are mine.

**Hands versus controllers**

- Hand tracking that works 90% of the time is not good enough. Table Troopers designed for hands but shipped early access without them: "It worked 90% of the time, but for it to be enjoyable, it has to work almost every time, or 99.9% of the time." [F] https://developers.meta.com/horizon/discover/success-stories/table-troopers/
- Their fixes: tint the hands red near the edge of the tracking volume, infer intent from hand position half a second before a release gesture, and simplify the mechanic instead of complicating the input. [F]
- Meta's guidance: tracking degrades when one hand hides the other; keep arms close to the body with elbows by the hips; put frequent actions within easy reach; confirm every pick-up with sight and sound; do not port controller interactions to hands. [F] https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands
- A fanned hand of cards held in one hand while the other picks from it is the worst case for hand occlusion (Inference from the guidance above). Shipped games avoid it: cards float in a tray or on the table and are pinched one at a time. Demeo lets players "pick up miniatures, play cards, and roll the die" by hand. [F]
- In Quest Browser the left palm pinch is reserved and exits the WebXR session; ordinary pinch arrives as a button press. [F] https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/webxr-hands/
- Hand input is the default on Android XR, and gaze-and-pinch is the default on Vision Pro, so a web game must work with pinch alone. [F]

**Dealing and card handling**

- Apple's TabletopKit models everything as equipment with parents: a card's parent is a deck, pile or hand; private hands are restricted by seat; moves are discrete validated actions synchronised in order, while animation runs locally. Apple suggests automating tedious steps such as dealing and keeping meaningful actions, such as drawing a card, manual. [F] https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10091/

**Table anchoring**

- On Quest, hit testing now works instantly from depth with no room scan (Quest 3/3S), which suits placing a board on a table; persistent anchors let a site remember the table, up to 8 per site. [F]
- Table Troopers keeps the real table "live": moving it moves the game. [F]

**Session length and comfort**

- Table Troopers players averaged 85 minutes a week in the first month, with "short sessions" a stated design pillar. [F]
- No sourced figure for ideal XR card-match length was found. Phone AR adds arm fatigue from holding the phone up (Inference): keep the tracked "comes alive" moment to under a minute and let the scene continue untracked.

**Text and voice in headsets**

- Quest Browser can raise the system keyboard inside an immersive session (browser 26.1+), with real limits: each opening starts a new edit that overwrites the old value, no key events, and the session goes "visible-blurred" while it is up. [F] https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/webxr-keyboard/ Composing a sonnet line by line on that keyboard would be poor.
- The Web Speech recognition API is "partial" in Chrome, Chrome for Android and Safari (including iOS 14.5+), unsupported in Edge and off by default in Firefox. [F] https://caniuse.com/speech-recognition Its behaviour in Quest Browser and Android XR Chrome is UNVERIFIED.
- Speaking verse is the natural headset input for this game (Inference), which makes the speech path, not the keyboard, the thing to test first on a real headset.

## 5. Gaussian splats and generated 3D scenes on the web

### 5.1 Viewers and formats

| Renderer | Facts | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Babylon.js** (already in the project) | Loads `.ply`, compressed PLY, `.splat`, Niantic `.spz` (v4), SOG, glTF with `KHR_gaussian_splatting`, and streamed SOG with levels of detail. Default splat budget: 2.5M desktop, 1.5M iOS, 1M other mobile. GPU sorting needs WebGPU and is opt-in | [F] https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/mesh/gaussianSplatting |
| **Spark 2.0** (World Labs, three.js, 14 Apr 2026) | WebGL2 by design. Continuous level of detail with a budget of 500K-2.5M splats by device; "most consumer devices render about 1 to 5M splats at interactive frame rates"; a 40M-splat demo streams on phones and runs on Quest and Vision Pro. New streaming format `.rad` | [F] https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/spark-2.0 |
| **PlayCanvas / SuperSplat** (3 Jun 2026) | WebGPU compute renderer with WebGL2 fallback. iPhone 13 Pro Max: 38 fps (WebGL2) vs 78 fps (WebGPU) at 1M splats; 20 vs 42 fps at 4M. Streamed SOG with automatic levels of detail. Viewer is MIT | [F] https://blog.playcanvas.com/new-in-supersplat-webgpu-and-streaming-bring-huge-performance-wins · https://github.com/playcanvas/supersplat-viewer |

- **Standard format:** Khronos published `KHR_gaussian_splatting` for glTF as a release candidate on 3 Feb 2026, with ratification expected in Q2 2026; SPZ compression is a proposed add-on. Whether ratification happened was not checked. [F] https://www.khronos.org/news/press/gltf-gaussian-splatting-press-release
- **Generated scenes:** World Labs' Marble (general availability 12 Nov 2025) generates 3D worlds from text, images or video and exports Gaussian splats, meshes and video; splats view in the browser through Spark. Pricing and licence terms were not on the page read. [F] https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/marble-world-model

### 5.2 Performance reading

- **Phones:** about 1M splats is the safe budget (Babylon's own default for mobile, and PlayCanvas's iPhone numbers). Mobile browsers cap GPU memory, which limits scene size. [F]
- **Headsets:** no published frame-rate benchmark for web splats inside a Quest WebXR session was found. A headset must draw two eyes at 72 Hz or more, so expect a much lower budget than on a phone (Inference). A PlayCanvas forum thread reports a visual bug with SOG in VR on Quest 3. [S] https://forum.playcanvas.com/t/sog-integration-for-webgl/40957 Treat splats in a headset as something to measure before promising.
- **File size:** use a compressed format (SOG or SPZ). A third-party guide claims roughly 95% size reduction for SOG; unverified. [S]

### 5.3 Card-triggered scenes

No shipped product that triggers a Gaussian-splat scene from a scanned physical card was found. UNVERIFIED as a negative. The components exist separately: per-card URL or QR, a splat file per card, and a viewer already in the project's engine. A small splat (a few hundred thousand splats) anchored over an image-tracked card is technically an ordinary 3D object to the renderer (Inference); nobody has documented doing it.

## 6. Co-located and face-to-face multiplayer on the web

**What the standards provide**

- The WebXR Anchors module (Editor's Draft, 27 Apr 2026) covers anchors and per-origin persistent anchors only. It says nothing about sharing anchors between devices. [F] https://immersive-web.github.io/anchors/
- Meta's Shared Spatial Anchors and Colocation Discovery, the features that power co-located Quest games such as Demeo's local mode, are documented for the native SDKs (Unity and others), with no WebXR path. [F] https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/unity-shared-spatial-anchors/ Meta's WebXR mixed-reality page lists passthrough, planes, anchors and persistent anchors, and no sharing. [F]
- Quest Browser will not give a page pose-aligned camera frames in AR, so a page cannot find a QR code on the table and align to it automatically. A user reports plain `getUserMedia` does return camera frames, without pose data. [F]
- Apple's shared experiences (SharePlay, spatial Personas, TabletopKit) are native only. [F]

**What is possible with no native SDK (Inference, built from the facts above)**

1. **Phones and a shared physical marker.** If two players each point a phone at the same printed card or play mat, each phone tracks the same physical image, so the virtual content is co-located by construction. No shared anchors are needed. Game state travels over an ordinary WebSocket or WebRTC room joined by QR code. This is the cheapest true face-to-face XR on the web.
2. **Headsets with manual alignment.** Each player marks the same two physical points (for example two corners of the table) with a pinch; the page computes a common frame and stores it in a persistent anchor. Workable on Quest and Android XR, clumsy, and drift is uncorrected.
3. **No spatial alignment at all.** Each headset player sees their own table with the opponent's cards mirrored. This is how remote play works anyway.

Face-to-face play does not need XR. Two people at a table with phones and physical cards are already co-located; the web only has to sync a match.

## 8. Implications for Sonnet Man

### The cheapest "card comes alive" moment on an ordinary phone

**A QR code (or short URL) on each card that opens that card's page, where the camera view tracks the card art and the character rises off it and speaks a line.** Built with camera-feed image tracking in JavaScript (MindAR or 8th Wall's MIT Image Targets), not WebXR.

- It works on iPhone and Android with no app, no fee and no store (section 3.3).
- It needs one compiled image target and one small animated model or splat per card.
- It falls back cleanly: if the camera is refused or tracking fails, the same page shows the scene inline with the phone's motion sensor moving the view.
- Two phones looking at the same card see the same thing, which gives face-to-face play for free (section 6).
- Cost risk: MindAR has no Babylon.js binding, so this page either uses three.js or needs glue code; tracking quality on glossy or dark card art must be tested on real prints.

### Stage 1: build now (web-first, phone-first)

| Feature | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Per-card URL and QR, with an inline 3D or splat scene | The reliable baseline that works on every device, including school iPads | 3.2, 7.3 |
| Camera-feed image tracking of the card ("comes alive") | Only app-free route covering iPhone | 3.2, 3.3 |
| Splat scenes capped near 1M splats on phones, in a compressed format, through Babylon's loader | Matches engine defaults and measured phone performance | 5.1, 5.2 |
| Face-to-face match by QR room join, physical or on-screen cards, state over WebSocket/WebRTC | Co-location needs no XR | 6 |
| Printable cards (PDF) that play with no server | The hybrids that survive keep the physical object useful when the service is gone | 2.2, 2.3 |
| Spoken verse on phones with typed fallback | Speech recognition is only "partial" everywhere | 4 |

### Stage 2: build next, behind feature detection

| Feature | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Quest and Android XR passthrough table mode (`immersive-ar`, hit test, anchors, pinch input) | Both browsers support it; the existing Babylon WebXR page is the base | 3.1 |
| Voice-first input in headset | The in-session keyboard is unfit for verse; verify speech recognition on device first | 4 |
| Vision Pro: flat Safari window plus `<model>` cards and an optional VR stage | Safari there has no AR | 3.2 |
| Horizon Store listing by packaging the web app | Discovery without a native build | 7.2 |
| Android-only WebXR floor placement of a full scene | Free extra where ARCore exists | 3.1 |

### Stage 3: defer

| Feature | Why |
|---|---|
| Co-located headset play with shared space | No shared anchors on the web; manual alignment is clumsy (6) |
| Fanned-hand card holding with hand tracking | Occlusion and the "99.9%" reliability bar (4) |
| Splats inside headset sessions | No performance data; measure first (5.2) |
| iOS through an App Clip | Third-party store asset with branding; revisit only if phone AR proves the demand (3.2) |
| Image-anchored AR Quick Look files for iPhone | Promising but untested on current iOS (3.2) |
| Smart glasses | Audio-only or tiny installed base; no card games exist (1.6) |

### Drop

| Idea | Why |
|---|---|
| "Each card unlocks an AR scene via WebXR on iPhone" | Not possible in Safari; no sign of change (3.2) |
| Passthrough AR on Vision Pro from the web | Apple says unsupported (3.2) |
| NFC chips in cards | No surviving non-crypto NFC card game; adds unit cost and inventory risk; QR does the job (2.2) |
| A digital layer the physical cards depend on (accounts, marketplace, card registry) | This is what killed Altered, Battlecast and Eye of Judgment's online play (1.7, 2.2) |
| Headset-first positioning or revenue expectations | Shrinking market, teen free-to-play audience, tiny numbers for the nearest comparable (7.1, 7.2) |
| WebXR image tracking as a shipping feature | Flag-only on one browser (3.3) |

### Funding fit

The work that funders pay for in this field is institution-led and free to the public (7.3). The realistic routes are a UK arts or games grant if there is a UK entity, or a partnership with a theatre, library or university that can lead a humanities bid. Meta's Start competition is worth a look in its next annual round only if the headset mode exists and is fully hand-tracked.
